The Theory of Narcissism and Bipolar

By Kyle Fournier

A lovely ex-girlfriend of mine recently got her degree in Psychology. She’s on the cutting edge. While there might be some therapists and psychiatrists that go deeper with their patients about the mechanisms of Bipolar Disorder, I’ve never spoken to one.

So when my ex-girlfriend told me her theory about the link between Narcissism and Bipolar Disorder, it shook my world.

It goes like this:

Those of us living with Bipolar have cripplingly large egos. We’re extremely selfish. As a result, our egos thrive on pride, success, and validation. These largely external factors - when they come - might be a serious trigger for hypomania/mania.

And if/when these external factors run dry, that void might devastate our self-images, sending us into depression.

This is NOT a New Diagnosis for You

Many of us struggling with Bipolar know the tangled web of diagnoses all too well. Some of us have been misdiagnosed multiple times and even gone through gauntlets of taking the wrong pills.

The truth is that the state of psychiatry and treatment today is pathetic, but that’s not the point of today’s post.

I would be remiss if I didn’t say this: I’m not talking about clinical Narcissistic Personality Disorder here. When I say “narcissism,” I’m talking about extreme egocentrism - the way our attention, self-talk, and focus become entirely consumed by ourselves, whether we feel like gods or pieces of garbage.

What Goes Up…

My Bipolar Bros co-founder Steve and I have always talked about feeling like “Superman” when we’re in hypomania/mania. We sleep and eat less - sometimes not at all - but we do more. We’re outgoing, smart, funny, creative, and productive. We go for what we want and we get it.

We achieve more in short periods of hypomania/mania than we do in long stretches of depression. I literally write thousands of words a day sometimes when I’m in hypomania/mania. That simply does not happen when I’m not.

I make friends, too. Talking to people is so easy when my mood’s up. I know exactly what to do and say and I’m not afraid to do it. This, too, is impossible when I’m depressed.

This ridiculous boost to our creativity, productivity, and confidence almost always leads to success which results in huge amounts of external validation. People love us and they praise what we do. It’s an upward spiral of positivity. We soar like eagles.

But we’re selfish, too. We’re always thinking about how to improve our lives and ourselves. We know how to talk to others, but we’re more concerned with our own self-talk than their lives. We listen when others tell us about their pain and hardship, but we don’t feel it. To us, the world is easy. So we might give them advice. We might tell them life is amazing. Because for us, it is.

For all intents and purposes, we’re narcissists thriving on inflated egos.

… Must Come Down

We can’t go at that pace forever. Nobody can. Eventually, the body needs rest and recovery, which it gets almost none of during hypomania/mania.

We crash.

That crash halts and/or stops our success. And without success, nobody’s telling us how awesome we are. We sleep more and sometimes eat more. We’re tired all of the time. Steve (somehow) manages to maintain his leanness during these times, but I get fat.

We stop working. We look at the projects we started and realize they’re impossible to continue and/or finish. We’re embarrassed by the way we conducted ourselves when we were way up in the sky.

We can’t maintain the new friendships and relationships we ignited during hypomania/mania. We don’t want to meet anyone. We can barely respond to texts.

We fall into depression.

And again, we’re selfish as fuck.

When others tell us of their success and happiness, we might congratulate them and feel a sliver of happiness for them, but it doesn’t really rub off. We’re consumed with our own negativity.

For all intents and purposes, we’re narcissists.

This is a Theory - a Perspective - Not a Law

My ex’s theory is extremely compelling. While I’m hesitant to fully subscribe, the concept fits my experience quite well. Can I say that I (fully) lack empathy? I don’t think so. But like most concepts in psychology, I don’t think this is black and white. I think it’s merely a new lens and perspective to look at Bipolar through.

The Problem: Warped Realities

What do new perspectives really give us? In my opinion, the real gems here are insight and awareness, which are the antidotes to many of our struggles.

Bipolar Disorder distorts our reality.

We’re not psychotic. We see the world fundamentally the same. But we view ourselves and our potentials through “rose-colored glasses,” for lack of a better term. Again, we think we’re “Superman.” We think we’ve finally found the answers to all of our problems and we think we’ll be able to implement our solutions and finally have the lives we’ve always dreamed of.

But it’s not true. The nature of Bipolar Disorder, of course, is moods that fluctuate - sometimes quite dramatically.

And when we fluctuate from high to low, our realities are warped the other way.

Again, we’re not psychotic. We see the world as it is, but we see it through “shit-covered glasses.” We think we see the truth of it all: we’re nothing and the world is hell. Every single positive thought, emotion, and idea of our time during hypomania/mania was a lie.

But both of those “truths” - that we’re superman and that we’re pieces of shit - are not truths. The self-images we created, both inflated and deflated, are wildly inaccurate. And our selfishness - our narcissism - makes our warped egos the center of our universes. That’s the real problem.

Increased Awareness Might be the Key

As far as I know, there’s no cure for Bipolar. And one of the central messages of Bipolar Bros is that we do not have the answer. So when I prescribe “awareness,” I’m not promising you the solution to all of your problems.

However, I think awareness might be a huge help.

If we practice awareness, we may be able to lessen the distortion of our realities during both hypomania/mania and depression. If we remind ourselves that we’re not Superman and we’re not pieces of shit, we might be able to stay more grounded. Rather than shooting into the stratosphere and plunging into the depths of hell, we might be able to slow down our ascents and descents - at least a little. And a little goes a long way.

This, Too, Shall Pass

I don’t remember where this saying comes from and I’m not gonna look it up right now. But it’s something Steve and I have repeated to ourselves and each other over the years. We’re so, so lucky to have each other. While we’ve actually accelerated each other’s hypomania/mania and depression when our moods lined up, we’ve also been able to remind each other that we’re Bipolar.

We see one another’s symptoms and sometimes acknowledge them out loud. When Steve tells me about the new project he’s working on relentlessly, I’ll ask him, “You’re not sleeping, are you?” When he inevitably tells me that he’s not, I tell him he might want to try.

We’re incredibly fortunate to be able to look at each other and see the mirror.

But awareness on its own is key, too.

On my own, even without Steve, I’ve learned to see my moods for what they are. I’m constantly checking myself. How’s my appetite, my energy, my libido? What do these variables indicate? And when I find that I’m shooting up or plunging down, I try to remind myself: “This, Too, Shall Pass.”

With that awareness, I can try and focus on myself less. I can try and be less selfish. I can try and care about others more.

I can be less narcissistic. And you can, too.

I’m Crashing RIGHT NOW

I wrote this whole damn thing in just a couple hours or less. That tells me that I’m at least a smidge manic. I’m also realizing that I’m running out of steam. The adderall and four coffees are wearing off.

I’d love to write more about Awareness and how to cultivate it, but that’s gonna have to wait for another post.

For now, I’m gonna check myself. I’m telling myself right now to be aware. I’m gonna stop writing and I’m gonna try and go out into the world and see it through a more balanced lens.

I’m going to stop focusing on myself and see others.

The Origin of the Bros

By Kyle Fournier

I met Steve in the fall of 2004 when I joined Coventry High School’s Cross-Country team. He was rude, obnoxious, mischievous - and vibrant. We got along immediately. I had no way of knowing - of course - that girl talk and dick jokes would change the entire course of my life.

If you’re not familiar with Cross-Country (XC, for short), it’s this very stupid “sport” in which you train by running a lot and then you compete by running a lot. But let’s back up for a moment.

How and Why I Started Running

In truth, I had no passion for running. I had dealt with weight problems and body dysmorphia ever since I could remember and after watching Rocky IV, I decided to start running over the summer break in between Freshman and Sophomore year. Along with a borderline anorexic diet and the magic of puberty, running daily helped me thin out dramatically.

The rewards of running outweighed its pain and monotony, so I kept at it. Then, it paid off during gym class. As a fat kid my entire life, I dreaded them. After months of training for an event that I never even considered, however, I cherished the opportunity to run some laps.
